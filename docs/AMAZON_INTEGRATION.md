## ASIN, product and offer identity

These are **M5 mapping choices**, tested locally and subject to source qualification; they are not claims about an Amazon identifier's guaranteed lifetime.

| Existing entity | M5 mapping and invariant |
|---|---|
| StoreInstance | Marketplace/site and access configuration. Fixture MX is isolated from future real MX and from US. Business customer/group pricing scope must not share anonymous or other-account evidence. |
| CanonicalProduct | Exact Pokémon set, commercial packaging/edition, language, sealed kind and sale-unit count. Manufacturer/GTIN evidence belongs in a reviewed mapping dossier. A title, image, ASIN or seller approval alone is insufficient. |
| StoreProduct | Marketplace-scoped ASIN. Each purchasable child ASIN is its own product. Price, seller, availability and URL tracking never enter this key. |
| Variant | `singleton:<child-ASIN>` under that exact StoreProduct. A catalog family does not force the child under a mutable Shopify-like parent. Parent/child relationships remain typed adapter metadata. |
| Listing | `detail:<ASIN>` represents the shared product detail presence. Multiple sellers can share it. A seller's own Listings Items SKU is a different concept and is not invented from this API. |
| Offer | Versioned synthetic tuple: ASIN, seller ID, requested condition, subcondition, normalized sealed-condition knowledge, fulfillment, delivery/pricing scope. Changing price or featured status does not change identity. Seller/fulfillment/condition/scope changes do. |
| Seller | Store-scoped external merchant ID. Display name is not identity. Missing ID produces an unresolved adapter finding and no fabricated offer. |

Catalog Items documents variation relationships using parent and child ASINs. The fixture proves that those relationships can be retained outside core and that parent-only results need not become purchase candidates. It does not prove a parent always remains retrievable or that an ASIN can never be merged/suppressed. [Catalog reference](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/catalog-items-api-v2022-04-01-reference).

Local URL normalization accepts bare ASINs, `/dp/<ASIN>`, `/title/dp/<ASIN>`, `/gp/product/<ASIN>` and `/gp/aw/d/<ASIN>` on HTTPS `amazon.com.mx`, `www.amazon.com.mx` or `m.amazon.com.mx`. It uppercases exactly ten alphanumeric characters and emits `https://www.amazon.com.mx/dp/<ASIN>`. This is syntax validation, not evidence that a product exists. Credentials, other hosts/countries, ports, short links and unsupported paths fail. Tracking query/fragment text is discarded; seller/offer/ASIN selection parameters are explicitly rejected rather than silently choosing another offer. No redirect or retail page is fetched.

Official Business offer IDs should be kept as source metadata until their lifetime and exact context are qualified. They must not replace the stable product key or be assumed to survive restocks. Its reference describes an offer identifier but does not establish immutable lifetime. [Business reference](https://docs.business.amazon.com/docs/product-search-api-v1-reference).

## Seller, fulfillment and product review

“Sold by Amazon” requires independently reviewed **MX merchant-ID evidence**, bound to the exact offer. M5 deliberately includes no real Amazon Retail merchant-ID allowlist. `SYNTHETIC_RETAIL` is an authored test identifier, not a credential or a claim about the real merchant.

“Ships from Amazon” / FBA produces a separate fulfillment reference (`fulfillment:amazon`); merchant fulfillment references the identified merchant. Unknown fulfillment stays UNKNOWN. Existing persistence already stores both seller and fulfillment references. The field model distinguishes these concepts; no core change is necessary. [Official Pricing v0 model](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/main/models/product-pricing-api-model/productPricingV0.json).

Existing `SellerValidationEngine` supplies APPROVED, REJECTED and REVIEW_REQUIRED (the specification's UNREVIEWED). An allowlist or independently reviewed first-party policy can approve; explicit deny takes precedence. Manual approval later means a **new policy revision and reevaluation**, not modifying historical observations. FBA, Prime, featured-offer selection, merchant name and reputation grant no approval. M5 uses ALLOWLIST; the existing general DENYLIST mode is not the default Amazon policy.

A separate authored review is the only way M5 supplies known commercial identity/sealed condition. It is bound to ASIN and a review reference; the actual `ProductMapping` must also be REVIEWED and match the canonical identity. This is scaffolding for the experiment, not a title classifier or a future live review workflow. English/Spanish, pack/box, bundle/ETB, count, import, resealed/open-box, individual card, preorder, accessory bundle, variation-count and title/identifier-conflict cases remain unreviewed without such evidence. Correctly reviewed wrong-language/count fixtures still fail matching. API “New” alone never proves factory sealed.

## Evidence and UNKNOWN handling

Parsing is bounded and consumes allowlisted fields only. The Node 24 JSON source-text reviver reads exact money lexemes before floating-point rounding; supported MXN amounts become integer minor units, and unsupported precision, currency or range fails. This is a deliberate parser subset, not a general JSON schema implementation.

Shipping availability `NOW` becomes an explicitly sourced **shipping-now stock signal**, not a reservation or exact quantity. Future shipping dates do not distinguish preorder, backorder and exhausted inventory reliably enough for this experiment; core stock stays UNKNOWN with an unavailable-for-shipping reason. Missing availability also stays UNKNOWN. An empty offer response is not evidence of global OUT_OF_STOCK: the official operation returns a bounded lowest-offer subset. [getItemOffers](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/getitemoffers).

Quoted shipping remains adapter metadata; buyer shipping cost, tax, exact available quantity and purchase limit stay UNKNOWN in the domain. Thus even approved, reviewed, apparently in-stock Amazon fixtures cannot create a simulated checkout attempt. A known zero API shipping quote is not promoted to a final delivered-cost fact without verified destination/account/quantity context.

No-offer, unidentified-seller and parent-only findings retain ASIN and typed metadata in the qualification result, with zero ListingObservations. The existing M2 repository writes full offer observations; it has no standalone catalog-absence history command. M5 does **not** fake an offer to fit it. Existing catalog/history rows survive subsequent unsuccessful reads. A durable catalog-only monitoring projection is a narrowly scoped candidate for the eventual adapter milestone, not an M5 schema migration or a present promise of restart-safe absence history.

Capture-derived observation IDs include version, ASIN, captured/source times, authored bodies and review evidence. Replaying one capture preserves its ID; changed values/times yield new observations. Stable entity keys omit time/price. Conflicting duplicate offer tuples fail instead of taking the lower price. Source timestamps never become fresh on replay. Raw bodies, unknown fields and response messages are not persisted.

## Restock semantics — qualified design, no notification implementation

The monitor's anchor is ASIN, independent of whether any seller offers it now. A meaningful change requires comparable source/interface version, marketplace, condition, fulfillment, seller-policy revision, destination/customer/group scope and quantity basis. Both snapshots must be successful, fresh, ordered, independently captured and complete for the **claimed** comparison. Repeated captures, auth errors, throttling, transport failures, partial pages or changed filters cannot establish absence or a restock.

| Comparison | Meaning; do not conflate |
|---|---|
| Same approved offer, authoritative OUT_OF_STOCK → IN_STOCK | True offer restock candidate only if that interface actually supplies both states with comparable coverage. This is not demonstrated by the v0 spike. |
| Successful scoped NO_OFFER → identified offer observed | Offer appeared in the response scope; not proof of a market-wide restock. |
| No approved observed offer → reviewed Amazon Retail/approved merchant observed | Approved-offer appearance; identify whether policy changed or actual offer evidence changed. An unreviewed FBA offer does not qualify. |
| Seller A → seller B, or FBA → merchant fulfillment | Offer substitution; invalidate old binding and evaluate the new seller/terms. |
| Featured selection changes | Featured-offer change, not necessarily stock change. |
| Price crosses a threshold while availability stays known | Price opportunity, not a restock. Missing costs still block eligibility. |
| API failure or censored/partial response | UNKNOWN observation gap; preserve the last fact with its age. |

Amazon Business documents an include-out-of-stock refinement, but unavailable-ASIN retrieval and transition fidelity still require live evidence. Business/customer filtering is part of the comparison scope. [Refinements](https://docs.business.amazon.com/docs/refining-search-results), [personalization](https://docs.business.amazon.com/docs/personalizing-searches).
